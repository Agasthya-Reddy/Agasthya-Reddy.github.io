Chemical-plume-tracking drone

An autonomous drone that finds the source of a gas leak or fire

When gas leaks or a fire starts, the first question is where it is coming from. I built an autonomous hexacopter that answers it. It carries an electronic nose and a biological sensor, and a Jetson Orin computer on board runs a reward-based machine learning algorithm that senses chemicals in the air and flies toward them until it reaches the source.

I also built the mapping software operators watch during a flight. It combines live ArduPilot flight data, GPS, and sensor readings into a 3D view of the plume as it forms, while Intel RealSense depth cameras help the drone spot and avoid obstacles along the way.

Built with
Jetson Orin, ROS2, PyTorch, ArduPilot, Intel RealSense depth cameras
What it does
Finds a chemical source on its own, avoids obstacles, and maps the plume in 3D

Passive radar using Starlink signals

Field-tested at T-REX 26-3, the Department of War's Technology Readiness Experimentation event

Regular radar sends out a signal and listens for the echo, which also gives away where the radar is. Passive radar sends nothing. I built the receiving system that picks out each Starlink satellite's Ku-band downlink channels, so we know which satellites are overhead.

Combined with passive radar dishes, that lets us detect drone swarms without transmitting anything ourselves. The system uses USRP B210 and RTL-SDR V4 radios with GNU Radio and SDR++, and a CFAR algorithm, which sets an adaptive detection threshold, separates real signals from background noise.

Result
Passive drone detection at distances up to 5 km
Signal processing
CFAR detection across 12 downlink channels spaced 250 MHz apart
Hardware
USRP B210, RTL-SDR V4, Kymeta Skyhawk u8 terminal

Camera and radar fusion for drone swarm detection

A tracking system that combines several sensors

To spot fast-moving drones by sight, I built a pan-tilt-zoom camera that runs its detection model locally on a Jetson AGX. For every drone it finds, it estimates size, speed, altitude, heading, and payload, and saves the track to a SQL database.

A custom model, served through an internal API, then combines the camera, radar, and telemetry data so the system can recognize a swarm in its late stages.

Result
96% effectiveness for late-stage swarm detection
Built with
Jetson AGX, SQL, a custom fusion model behind an internal API

Long-range LoRa telemetry module

Solo build: a tracker that reports its position from kilometers away

I designed a small, self-contained tracker you can strap onto almost anything that moves, even something with no telemetry of its own. It sends its full position data back over a long-range LoRa radio link from kilometers away.

It combines GPS, barometer, and IMU readings to place the asset in 3D space, runs on custom firmware, and plugs into any laptop over serial.

Result

Built with
Custom STM32 firmware, LoRa, GPS, barometer, IMU

Blood pressure monitor

Best in Class. Mostly analog electronics, accurate to within 5% of a commercial cuff

I built a blood pressure monitor from scratch, mostly out of analog electronics. Analog circuits amplify and filter the tiny pressure signal from the cuff, a microcontroller turns it into systolic, mean, and diastolic readings, and the results appear wirelessly on any phone. It took months of troubleshooting and testing to get within 5% of a commercially available cuff.

The phone interface includes a chatbot, built on a regression tree (CART), that answers questions about your current and past readings with guidance based on medical guidelines. The whole thing runs on 512 KB of memory and needs no internet, which helps in remote areas.

Result
Readings within 5% of a commercial device
Signal chain
INA126P instrumentation amplifier, cascaded Sallen-Key filters, gain and DC bias
Software
Oscillometric algorithm for systolic, mean, and diastolic pressure, plus an offline assistant in 512 KB of RAM
